01

Foundations

What the product is, and the one constraint everything else answers to.

The product

Vitae is a private, single-operator life and career record: a typed, versioned graph of people, roles, projects and artifacts, plus a deterministic engine that compiles that graph into a résumé. It holds many people’s records, not only the owner’s. There is no AI at runtime; every ranking, score and suggestion comes from an algorithm that will give the same answer twice.

Three properties follow from that, and the interface has to show all three: it is an archive (things persist and accumulate), it is versioned (the same record has a history), and it is explainable (every number decomposes into the facts that produced it).

The governing constraint: calm. This is a tool someone works inside every day, not a page they visit once. Nothing glows, nothing shimmers, nothing gradients, nothing moves for atmosphere, and no large area is saturated. If a screen pulls the eye somewhere, that somewhere had better be what the user came for.

Principles

Brightness is the accent

There is no accent hue. Emphasis comes from lightness and weight; the active thing is simply the brightest thing. This is the whole idea. Everything else follows from it.

Colour is spent on data

The entire colour budget goes to the categorical ramp and to semantic states, where colour encodes something. Chrome stays achromatic.

Data is monospace

IDs, timestamps, counts, scores and provenance are always mono with tabular figures, so columns align and numbers can be compared by eye.

Structure carries meaning

Rules, ticks, indents and numbering encode something true about the content. If a divider isn’t saying anything, delete it.

Every score decomposes

No number appears without a way to see what produced it. Ranking is a promise the interface has to keep.

Keyboard first

Capture, search and navigation are reachable without the mouse, and the shortcut is shown next to the control, not hidden in a help page.

  • Ember is streaks and celebration only. It is not a second accent and it never marks an error or a warning.

Categorical ramp

Graph node types and chart series. Validated against both #0F0F0F and #232323: lightness band, chroma floor, adjacent-pair colourblind separation, normal-vision separation and contrast all pass on each surface.

Slots are assigned in fixed order and never cycled. A ninth series folds into “Other”, becomes small multiples, or gets a second encoding; it is never a generated hue. All-pairs separation does not pass at eight slots (no eight-way categorical set does), so identity is never colour-alone: every type also carries its glyph shape and a direct label.

Borders: there are three

TokenHexvs canvasUsed for
--ae-border#2B2B2B1.35Decorative hairlines only: between rows, under headers, around cards
--ae-border-strong#3F3F3F1.82Emphasis, chip outlines, scrollbars
--ae-border-control#7272713.98Any border that is the only thing defining a control
WCAG 2.1 §1.4.11 requires 3:1 for non-text UI. A hairline between two rows is decoration and may sit below that. The outline of a text input, a checkbox, or a secondary button is the control’s boundary and must use --ae-border-control, which clears 3:1 on all four surfaces (3.98 on canvas, 3.26 on overlay). The previous identity used a 1.3:1 hairline for both jobs; that was a bug, and this split is the fix.

  • Headings get text-wrap: balance.

06

Layout & surfaces

Dense but not cramped. The app is a working instrument: more rows visible beats more whitespace, up to the point where scanning gets hard.

Spacing

4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 32 · 48

Seven steps, no others. Gaps come from flex/grid gap, never from per-element margins that collapse or double.

Radii

2 · 3 · 5 · 999

Deliberately tight. Large radii are the single strongest signal of a generated card grid; 3px reads as a cut edge, not a pill.

Density

row 36px · control min 32px

List rows are 36px. Interactive targets are at least 32×32, and never below the 24×24 floor in chapter 12.

Elevation

Elevation is tone plus a hairline. Move up the surface ramp and add a 1px border. Dialogs and popovers may carry one soft shadow to separate them from the page. Nothing else may, and no shadow may be visible as a shadow.

Page anatomy

Every screen inside the shell is built the same way, so navigation feels like one instrument rather than thirty pages.

Breadcrumb ⌘K

The register rule: the signature

A hairline carrying small ticks, drawn under every page header. It is structural, not decorative: the ticks encode something true about what is on the page.

ScreenWhat a tick means
A recordOne revision of that record. The active tick is the version being viewed
The shellThe section’s position within its nav group
Résumé outputsOne compiled output from the current target
TimelineA decade boundary in the visible range
Ticks are drawn in --ae-border-strong; only the active tick takes the accent. If a screen has nothing true to encode, it gets a plain rule with no ticks. Never fake them.

Breakpoints

WidthBehaviour
< 640Single column; nav becomes a bottom dock; tables become stacked rows

07

Backgrounds

This is the shortest chapter in the book, and that is the point.

Backgrounds are flat. A surface is one of the five values in chapter 03 and nothing else. No texture, no noise, no grain, no grid, no gradient, no image, no ambient wash, no glow, no backdrop-filter.

Two of the three rejected directions carried a background treatment (a paper grain and a graph-paper rule grid), and both were defensible. This one deliberately has none, because texture is exactly the kind of thing that looks considered in a specimen and becomes noise on the four hundredth screen of the day.

The one exception is the marketing surface at /welcome, which may carry a single static graphic in the hero. It stops at the fold and never appears behind the application.

Instead of decoration

  • An empty region gets type and a rule: a title, a sentence, and the action that fills it. See chapter 10.
  • A large blank area is a layout problem, not a place to add an illustration.
  • Dialog backdrops are a flat rgba(0,0,0,0.66): dimmed, never blurred.
08

Motion

Restrained, not absent. Motion exists to report that something changed, never to decorate, never to fill time, never to make the product feel “alive”.

The test. Before adding an animation, name the state change it reports. If you can’t, delete it. If the user would not notice its absence, delete it.

Tokens

TokenValueUse
--ae-dur-fast90msExits, hover, anything leaving
--ae-dur130msDefault: entrances, emphasis, route swaps
--ae-dur-slow220msReorder and layout shifts only
--ae-easecubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1)Everything. There is one easing.

Exits use linear: an element that is leaving should not decelerate, it should just go.

The vocabulary

Five named variants live in apps/web/src/lib/motion.ts. Nothing outside this list gets animated without adding it to the list first.

1234
enter · 130ms · opacity + 4px rise · stagger 25ms
123
exit · 90ms · opacity only · linear
idlechangedidle
emphasis · 130ms · brightness only, no movement
ABC
reorder · 220ms · transform only (FLIP)
pagecontent
route · 130ms · opacity only, no movement

Choreography

  • Stagger is capped: 25ms step, at most 5 children, 125ms total. The sixth item onward appears with the fifth.
  • Only one thing animates per interaction. If a row updates and a toast appears, the row is the one that moves.
  • Travel is short: 4px for entrances. Nothing slides across the screen except a reorder, which moves the distance it actually moved.
  • No spring, no overshoot, no bounce, no elastic.
  • No shimmer sweep, no gradient animation, no pulsing glow.
  • Route changes never slide. A page that slides in implies spatial relationships between screens that don’t exist.
  • Nothing loops. The only repeating animation in the product is the skeleton opacity pulse, and it stops the moment content arrives.

Patterns

EventMotion
Route changeroute on the content region only. The shell never animates.
List loadsenter, staggered, first five rows only
Row saved (optimistic)emphasis on the row, then the toast appears with enter
Row deletedexit, then the list closes the gap with reorder
Dialog opensenter at 130ms; backdrop fades at the same time. No scale.
Dialog closesexit at 90ms
Skeleton → contentSkeleton stops, content enters. Never cross-fade the two.
Sort or filter changesreorder. Rows move; they do not re-enter.
Reduced motion. prefers-reduced-motion: reduce sets all three duration tokens to 0ms at the root, and every variant in motion.ts reads useReducedMotion. State changes still happen - they just happen instantly. Nothing is left mid-transition and nothing becomes invisible.

Primary · ghost · quiet · danger · disabled

10

Feedback & states

The user should never wonder whether something worked. Every action gets an answer, in words, within 100ms.

The four-state contract. Every screen and every data region ships all four: loading, empty, error, populated. A screen missing one of them is unfinished, and this is checked in review.

The four states

StateWhat it looks like
LoadingSkeletons in the shape of the content that is coming: same row height, same column count. Never a centred spinner on a full page.
EmptyA title, one sentence of orientation, and the action that fills it. Distinguish “nothing here yet” from “nothing matched your filter”; they need different actions.
ErrorWhat failed, in the interface’s voice, plus a Retry. Technical detail goes in mono underneath, not in the sentence.
PopulatedThe real thing.

Mutations

  • Optimistic by default. Apply the change locally, mark the row with emphasis, then reconcile. On failure, roll back visibly and say why.
  • Every mutation produces a toast whose verb matches the button (“Publish” → “Published”).
  • Anything destructive or hard to redo offers Undo in the toast, wired to a real compensating call, not a client-side trick.
  • Nothing may fail silently. The global mutation handler is the backstop, not the plan.

Error boundaries

The app currently has no error boundary anywhere. A render error in one panel blanks the whole page. The target is two levels: one around the shell that keeps navigation alive, and one per route that fails to a retryable panel while the rest of the app keeps working. Each reports what broke and offers a way back.

Latency

ElapsedResponse
< 100msNothing. Show the result.
100-400msDisable the control and change its label. No skeleton (it would flash).
> 400msSkeletons in the content region
> 5sSay what is taking time and offer a way out (“Still compiling: you can keep working”)

Content & voice

Words are design material. They are the fastest way to make the product feel considered and the fastest way to make it feel generated.

Voice

  • Write from the user’s side of the screen. Name things by what they recognise: a person manages connections, not edges.
  • Active voice, imperative controls. A control says exactly what happens when it is used.
  • Specific beats clever. “No contact in 94 days” beats “It’s been a while!”.
  • One job per element. A label labels; an example demonstrates; nothing quietly does both.
  • No exclamation marks. No “Oops”, “Whoops”, “Sorry”. Errors don’t apologise, they explain.
  • No emoji in product surfaces.

12

Accessibility

WCAG 2.1 AA is the floor, not the goal. These are the rules that get checked on every screen before it ships.

Contrast

  • Text clears 4.5:1 measured against the lightest surface it can appear on, not the canvas. Every ink and semantic token in chapter 03 is verified this way.
  • Non-text UI clears 3:1: that is what --ae-border-control exists for. Decorative hairlines are exempt because they carry no information.
  • Chart marks clear 3:1 against the chart surface, verified on both the canvas and the overlay.
  • Never communicate with colour alone. Every state carries a glyph and a word as well.

Focus & keyboard

2px ring in --ae-accent, offset 2px in --ae-bg
  • Focus is always visible. outline: none without a replacement ring is never acceptable.
  • Every action is reachable by keyboard. Shortcuts are shown next to the control (⌘K capture, ⌘P switcher, n new record).
  • Dialogs trap focus, close on Esc, and return focus to the element that opened them.
  • A skip link precedes the nav rail. The app has none today, and it is part of the shell work.
  • Tab order follows visual order. If it doesn’t, the DOM order is wrong, not the tabindex.

Announcements & targets

  • Toasts and async results are role="status"; errors are role="alert". Neither is announced twice.
  • Interactive targets are at least 32×32, and never below the 24×24 WCAG 2.2 floor.
  • Icon-only controls carry an accessible name and a tooltip with the same words.
  • Reduced motion is honoured everywhere (chapter 08).
  • The document has one h1, and heading levels never skip.
13

Data visualisation

This is where the colour budget goes. Charts are read and operated, so they get the same care as type.

Colour by job

JobRule
Categorical (identity)The eight-slot ramp, fixed order, never cycled
Sequential (magnitude)One hue, light → dark. Never a rainbow.
Diverging (polarity)Two hues with a neutral grey midpoint, never a hue at the middle
Status (state)The semantic five. Reserved: never reused as a series.
Colour follows the entity, never its rank. A filter that changes the series count must not repaint the survivors.

Marks & anatomy

  • Thin marks: 2px lines, markers at least 8px, a 2px surface gap between adjacent fills and stacked segments.
  • Grid and axes are recessive: --ae-border at most, and often nothing.
  • A legend is present for two or more series; up to four are also directly labelled. One series needs no legend: the title names it.
  • Never a number on every point. Label the endpoints and the extremes.
  • Values, labels and legends wear text tokens, never the series colour. A coloured mark beside them carries the identity.
  • Never a dual-axis chart. Two measures of different scale become two charts, small multiples, or an indexed common base.

Interaction

  • Crosshair and tooltip on line and area charts; per-mark tooltip on bar, dot and cell. Only a bare stat tile skips this.
  • Hit targets are larger than the marks.
  • Filters sit in one row above the chart, never inside it.
  • A table view of the same data is always reachable.
Before shipping any new palette, run the validator rather than reasoning about it - lightness band, chroma floor, adjacent-pair colourblind separation, normal-vision separation and contrast, against both #0F0F0F and #232323. The current ramp passes all five on both.

Adding a token

  • Add it to tokens.css with a comment saying why the value is what it is. The existing file documents its own contrast reasoning, and that is the standard.
  • If it is a colour, record its measured contrast against the lightest surface it can appear on, in the comment.
  • Alias it in theme.css so a utility exists.
  • Update this document. A token that isn’t in the guide will drift.
  • Never add a token to solve one screen. Ask whether the screen is wrong first.
